# Background Context — Wai Wai / CG Foods

Use this as the reference for the "known events" to overlay on the sentiment timeline, and for framing the analysis narrative.

## Company & Brand

Wai Wai is the flagship instant noodle brand of CG Foods (Nepal), part of the Chaudhary Group — Nepal's largest conglomerate. Wai Wai has long held market-leader status in Nepal and an especially dominant position in North East India, where it reportedly holds roughly 80% of the instant-noodle market. Nationally in India, Wai Wai is the third-largest instant noodle brand (behind Nestlé's Maggi, which holds roughly 60% share, and ITC's Sunfeast Yippee!), with an estimated ~27% share driven mostly by its North East stronghold. CG Foods India has been on a strong growth trajectory: revenue rose from roughly Rs 500 crore (FY23–24) to Rs 800 crore (FY24–25), with a target of Rs 1,200 crore for FY25–26.

## The Nepal Revenue Decline

CG Foods' Nepal business, by contrast, has seen revenue pressure. Noodles account for roughly 76% of the company's total earnings (H1 FY2024/25), and that segment has softened as Nepali consumer taste has shifted toward spicier noodle products. Domestic rivals **Current Noodles** and **2PM Noodles** have gained meaningful traction in this "spicy noodle" category, eating into Wai Wai's home-market position even as its India business grows. This split — declining at home, growing abroad — is a useful framing device for the sentiment analysis: check whether Nepali-sourced sentiment differs materially from India-sourced sentiment.

## Known Events to Correlate Against the Sentiment Timeline

When building the sentiment-over-time chart, check for dips/spikes around these known events (confirm exact dates during data collection, as reporting dates vary):

- **Price hikes.** Nepali instant noodle makers, including Wai Wai, raised retail prices by roughly 20% citing weakening of the Nepali currency against the US dollar, higher raw material costs, and rising labor/wage costs. Wai Wai (through CG's Thai operations) has also sought/implemented price increases in Thailand tied to wheat flour, palm oil, and energy cost spikes. Price-hike periods are a strong candidate for sentiment dips, particularly in "complaint" theme volume around price.
- **Quality/health controversy (2024).** Kathmandu Metropolitan City fined Chaudhary Group after testing found Wai Wai noodles did not meet quality standards (acid value out of spec), raising public health concerns. This is a likely sharp negative-sentiment spike — check comment volume and negative keyword frequency (quality, health, safety) around this period.
- **Trademark/competitor litigation (2024).** Patan High Court, and later a higher court, ordered Yashoda Foods' "Current Noodles" to stop imitating Wai Wai's trademark/packaging. This dispute itself may generate chatter (brand-protection sentiment, or backlash if perceived as Wai Wai using legal muscle against a popular local challenger) — worth checking as a secondary event.
- **Competitor launches/gains.** Rising popularity of Current Noodles and 2PM Noodles in the spicy segment — track whether comments increasingly mention these brands by name in a comparative/negative context ("switched to Current Noodles because...").
- **India market expansion.** Wai Wai's aggressive India growth push and increased retail presence — a candidate for positive-sentiment growth in India-sourced content, independent of the Nepal narrative.

## Recurring Praise & Complaint Themes (for word-cloud/keyword design)

- Praise: taste/flavor, nostalgia ("grew up eating this," "childhood snack"), availability/ubiquity, price-to-value historically, spice packet quality.
- Complaints: price increases, reduced spice-packet quantity or heat level ("not as spicy as before"), packaging changes, perceived quality/ingredient changes, availability gaps in certain regions, competitor comparisons.
